# Manual tests

Behavior that the automated tests (`python tools/run_tests.py`) don't cover and that has to be checked by a person. The automated tests cover how the project is put together (it opens on the shooter level with the game's own mode, the template's Blueprints load against our C++ classes, nothing any asset needs is missing), the rules as plain functions (movement numbers, weapon damage and spread, where the killed come back, the scoreboard's order, the kill limit), and, playing the game in the editor (`CheeseStrike.Play.*`), that the character walks, sprints, slides, crouches and jumps at the right speeds, that the weapon fires, aims, empties and reloads by its numbers, that a character carries a primary, the pistol and the knife and changes between them by their keys (each with its own rounds and draw time, a chosen primary carried from the next life, the server following the change, the pistol's and the knife's damage and the stroke in the back, the modes' one-slot and one-weapon calls), every weapon's numbers against the design's table, and that a far shot is heard quieter and duller, and that over the network a kill is scored and shown, the fallen body comes to rest on both machines with every joint within its ragdoll's limits, the chest not twisted round on the hips and the face in place, the killed come back at a start with full health and protection, a match and each later round count in (3, 2, 1, FIGHT) with everyone held where they stand, the count the same on a joiner's machine and its tones sounded, protection keeps a fresh spawn unhurt and ghostly (its body, arms and weapon) until it runs out or the player fires, strikes or calls in a streak, on every machine, and the match ends at the kill limit with a winner; the modes and streaks as plain functions (what each mode is, who takes a round, who may hurt whom, where a side comes in, when streaks are earned, where a Cheese Strike's wheels land and what they do), and, played, that a team wins Deathmatch at its kill limit with friendly fire off, that a round of Elimination ends when a side is out and the next brings everyone back, and that the three streaks are called in (from the host and from a joiner) and score for their callers; Execution's last stand, executions, getting up alone and Gun Game's ladder as plain functions, and the rules of lying down (the cone, the turning, the scoot, where the body lies, a shot at a body lying down); and, played, that in Execution a player shot down goes down with only the pistol, lying on their back on every machine (the pelvis on the floor, the feet out ahead of it, propped on the left elbow with the head raised over the chest, the pistol held out ahead, the view low and dark), the server's shots finding the body where it lies and the eyes at the raised head, the view kept to a cone toward the feet with the body turning round after it slowly and the scoot at its speed, and shown down in the row along the top, is revived by a teammate holding E and goes down again (not killed) when shot down again, after a revive or getting up alone, an enemy's single press of E executes someone down (every machine seeing it, the executor beside them, unhurt by any shot, its view pulled out and back), someone down fires a far weaker pistol, someone left down gets up by themself after 20 s with little health, a side all down stays in until each is executed, the rounds and the match still ending, and that a bot goes to execute an enemy down from outside their aim and revives a teammate down, and that everyone comes in with the weak Colby Carbine and takes a better primary from the maps' stands (walked over with the Carbine, pressed for with Interact otherwise), the stand empty on every machine until it comes back, with Gun Game having none and bots going for them, and that every map has four to eight stands fair to both sides, that in Gun Game a player carries only the step's weapon, the keys to change weapons doing nothing, a kill moves the killer up the ladder at once and a knife kill on the last step wins; King of the Hill's rules as plain functions (who holds the hill, alone or contested, in teams and solo, the points, its round of spots, where players come back, what a bot does about it, what the HUD says), and, played, that a side scores while it alone holds the hill and not while it is contested, to the target, that the hill warns, moves to its next spot and players come back away from it, that bots go to the hill and hold it, and that every map's hill spots are fair, reached from both sides and on floors the mice never eat; that a hit reaches the hurt player's machine as a damage arc pointing at the shooter; and the HUD's rules as plain functions (`CheeseStrike.HUD.*`: how the minimap turns, cuts its shapes and picks what to show by height, which way an arc points, how red the edges get, what the row of players and the score summary say, how name tags shrink, how the death screen's cheese runs). How the HUD looks is checked here.

**Keeping this current:** when a change adds or alters behavior that an automated test can't cover, add or update a row here in the same pull request. When a row becomes covered by an automated test, delete it.

**What to run:** the sections for whatever a change touches. Run everything before sharing a build with other people.
