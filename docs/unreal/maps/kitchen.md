# Crumb Kitchen

A giant's kitchen, five times life size, so that the players are the size of a mouse in a real one: about 70 by 46 m and 12.5 m to the ceiling, for up to sixteen players. A sprint from one side's starts to the other's takes about 10 seconds by the floor, longer by the worktop. It is built by `tools/art/build_kitchen.py` into `Content/CheeseStrike/Maps/Lvl_Kitchen`, from the blocks in `tools/art/map_kit.py` ([principles.md](principles.md) says how maps are made). Its signature is scale: familiar things made huge (a toaster is crouching cover, a stack of cookbooks is a staircase, the table is a roof over a hall of legs), and a wedge of cheese the size of a house leaning on the counter, which the mice eat down to its red wax rind.

## The art

A real kitchen, not a cartoon one: oak worktop, chequered marble floor tiles, white metro tiles behind the worktop, painted cupboards with steel handles, plastered walls and ceiling, a stainless sink with its tap, a stove, a washing machine, a steel fridge and a painted larder, a farmhouse table and its chairs, and the window over the sink open onto a garden with a brick wall, through which the sun comes in across the room. Its surfaces and things are from [Poly Haven](https://polyhaven.com) (CC0), drawn at five times their real size (a 30 cm floor tile is 1.5 m): textures for the worktop, floor, tiles, walls, cupboards, table, garden and book covers; models for the kettle, microwave, stove, chopping boards, pans and pots, the jug, tea set, bowl and fruit, bread, cake, groceries, onions, cleaning bottles, buckets, the bin and the lamps over the table. They come the maps' one way for Poly Haven: `tools/art/polyhaven.py` downloads them (with ordinary Python) and `tools/art/import_polyhaven.py` imports them (in the editor) and makes their materials as new assets only (`MI_Kitchen_*`, instances of `M_Surface`; `MI_<model>`, instances of `M_Prop`). `tools/art/kitchen_assets.py` makes the rest of the kitchen's own art: stainless steel and enamel (the maps' painted metal, recoloured), and the books' pages. It also makes `SM_KitchenBook`, a book whose covers and spine take one material (leather, dyed) and its pages another, from which the stairs of cookbooks are piled. The toaster, grater, knife block, bread bin, washing machine, dresser and cupboards are blocks in those materials. The wedge's slope is the template's ramp mesh (`LevelPrototyping/Meshes/SM_Ramp`) in cheese, so it is a smooth slope you walk up, not a stair.

Its places are named (`ACheeseStrikePlace`): The Fridge and The Larder, The Worktop, The Hob, The Kettle, The Toaster, The Knife Block, the Gouda and Mozza Boards, The Sink, the Gouda and Mozza Cookbooks, Cupboards, Drawers and Crumbs, Under the Sink, The Wedge, The Hollow, The Crumbs, Under the Table, The Skirting (the south wall's middle), The Bin and The Groceries.

The lanes: the worktop (high, along the north wall, cover of appliances and cheese), the floor (the middle, round the wedge and through its hollow), and the table (low and covered underneath, high ground on top). The connectors: the wedge's slope (floor to worktop, in the middle, until the mice eat it; then its two waxed rinds), the cookbooks at each end, the cupboard doors (floor to the cupboards, and from the hollow to Under the Sink), the cupboards themselves once their seals are eaten (a covered way under the worktop from one side to the other), and the books up the table's ends.

Free-for-all has twelve more starts (tagged "Free"), six a side: on the worktop, in the cupboard, under the table, in front of the drawers, by the south wall and in the crumbs.

The mice come out of holes (`MouseEntry`, in the level's `Mice` folder, each drawn as a dark hole): four in the skirting of the south wall, two in the splashback on the worktop, and one at the back of each side's cupboard. Five come (one to every sixty of its 308 blocks), at five of the eight.

The bots and the mice walk a navigation mesh built when the level starts, in the bounds of its `Navigation` volume: up the cookbooks and the wedge to the worktop, through the cupboard doors, up the books onto the table, down into the sink and out.

Light: day, the sun low through the north window, across the lanes, falling on the sink and the wedge; the lamps over the table, under the wall cupboards, in the cupboards and in the hollow.

## The cheese, and what the mice change

There are 308 blocks the mice can eat, and 36 they can't (the wedge's rind). Twelve mice eat it all in about a minute and a quarter, at the mice's steady pace: the floor's cheese and the wedge first, the cupboards' too; the cheese up on the worktop and the table last, since the mice come to it last (it is high, and few of them come in up there). What each structure does before the mice, and what eating it does (`tools/play/kitchen_mice.txt` watches it happen):

| Structure | Blocks | Before | Eaten |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Wedge** | 152 | A ramp of cheese from the floor to the worktop in the middle, the middle's high ground and its way up; the hollow under its top end, a dark covered way along the counter from one side to the other. | The middle opens: the floor runs under the worktop from end to end, and the middle's way up is gone except for the rinds. |
| **WedgeRind** | 36, never eaten | The wedge's sides, red wax. | Two narrow red ridges from the floor to the worktop, each 1.3 m wide, bridging over where the hollow was: the middle's way up becomes two balance walks. |
| **HollowPillar** | 2 | A pillar in the hollow's middle: you squeeze past it either side, and no line runs along the counter. | The hollow is one line from end to end, until the wedge over it goes too. |
| **CupboardSeal** | 8 | The holes between the cupboards are stopped: each side's cupboard is a dead end off the floor. | The cupboards join up under the worktop: a covered way from one side's cupboard to the other's through Under the Sink, and from the floor to the hollow. |
| **CupboardGouda**, **CupboardMozza** | 10 each | Wheels along each cupboard's back and a block by its door: cover inside. | Bare cupboards. |

| **CheddarGouda**, **CheddarMozza** | 8 each | A block of cheddar on the floor in front of the drawers: full cover. | The floor in front of the cupboard doors is open. |
| **CrumbsGouda**, **CrumbsMozza** | 16 each | Crumbs across the floor: crouching cover every few metres. | The floor is bare tiles. |
| **UnderTableGouda**, **UnderTableMozza** | 11 each | A lump fallen under the table, and crumbs: cover among the legs. | Under the table is a hall of legs. |

So the kitchen starts closed (a wedge in the middle, cupboards that are dead ends, cheese on every surface) and opens as a match goes on: the middle loses its wedge but keeps two red ridges, the cupboards join into a way under the worktop, the floor is bare, and the worktop and the table, whose cheese lasts longest, become the high ground over an open room. What anyone stands on is never eaten from under them except the wedge's slope; the floor, the worktop, the books, the table and the rinds stay.

## King of the Hill

King of the Hill's hill goes round four spots (`hills()`, `ACheeseStrikeHillSpot`), a minute at each: **the Crumbs** between the wedge's tip and the table, **Gouda's drawers** in front of the counter, **the table's top** (3.75 m up, reached by the books at its ends) and **Mozza's drawers**. The Crumbs and the table are on the middle line and the drawers are twins; all are on the room's floor or the table, never eaten.

## Tests and tooling

`CheeseStrike.Play.EveryMapHasFairHills` checks its hill spots: three to five, each on a floor the mice never eat, clear and level round its middle, on the navigation mesh and reached from both sides' starts, well away from them, the first as far from both and the round favouring neither. `CheeseStrike.Play.CrumbKitchenHoldsTogether` plays the map: starts for both sides, every structure there, its size, the lines above (the pillar closes the hollow, the slope and its rind, a slot open into the hollow, the seal closes the cupboards, the table's top roofs the floor, the cheese on the table and on the boards is cover), that no line from the room (the floor, the worktop, the table top, on a grid) reaches either side's starts, with the cheese and without it, and a walk up both cookbooks, up the wedge, along the hollow and through its door under the sink, through a cupboard's door, up the books onto the table, and down into the sink and out. `CheeseStrike.Play.BotsPlayCrumbKitchen` checks a way on the navigation mesh from a side's starts to every place and watches fifteen bots go up onto the worktop and fight across the floor and the table. `CheeseStrike.Play.AfterTheMice.CrumbKitchen` eats all its cheese and checks every start has ground and a way to every other. `tools/play/kitchen_tour.txt` takes pictures of it from set places, and `tools/play/kitchen_mice.txt` pictures from the corners as the mice eat it, with bots fighting (`tools/play.ps1 -Map /Game/CheeseStrike/Maps/Lvl_Kitchen`, and `-Bots -Mice -Extra "?MiceAt=5?KillLimit=80" -TimeoutSeconds 600` for the mice; from Git Bash, set `MSYS_NO_PATHCONV=1` so the path isn't rewritten). Its lobby picture is `python tools/art/map_picture.py /Game/CheeseStrike/Maps/Lvl_Kitchen kitchen.png 2450 -2000 1170 -24 132 --start -3300,200,120,0`.

Changing the map is a change to `tools/art/build_kitchen.py`, then running it (it rebuilds the level from nothing) and the tests; a new texture or model is a line in `tools/art/polyhaven.py` and `tools/art/import_polyhaven.py`, run before the map's script (the first outside the editor, the second in it).
